## What "Best Tandoori Chicken" Actually Means

**Tandoori chicken** is chicken marinated in yoghurt, ginger-garlic paste,
and a dry spice blend, then roasted in a **tandoor** — a cylindrical clay
oven that reaches 400–500°C. The high, dry heat chars the surface quickly
while keeping the inside juicy, without needing any oil.

People searching "best tandoori chicken near me" are usually looking for one
or more of these things:

- **Authenticity** — real clay-tandoor cooking, not an electric grill dressed up as one
- **Freshness** — cooked to order, not sitting under a heat lamp
- **Value** — fair pricing for the portion size
- **Proximity** — something close enough to grab on a weeknight

A restaurant that's genuinely good at tandoori chicken usually gets all four
right at once — because the process itself doesn't allow shortcuts.

## How Real Tandoori Chicken Is Made

### The Marinade

Good tandoori chicken starts 12+ hours before it's cooked. The chicken is
scored (so the marinade penetrates, not just coats) and submerged in:

- Whisked yoghurt (tenderises the meat and carries the spices)
- Ginger-garlic paste
- Red chilli powder, cumin, coriander, and a house spice blend
- A small amount of mustard or vegetable oil

![Marinated tandoori chicken pieces ready for the clay tandoor](/classic-chicken-tandoori.jpg "Overnight yoghurt-and-spice marinade, before it goes into the tandoor")

### The Tandoor

The marinated chicken goes onto iron skewers and straight into the clay
tandoor. At 450°C, the outside chars and seals in moisture within minutes —
there's no oil bath, no basting, no frying at any stage. This is the step
that separates real tandoori chicken from anything cooked in a pan or a
deep-fryer.

> "It's roasted, not fried. That's not a slogan — it's the entire menu."

### Serving

Tandoori chicken is served hot, straight off the skewer, usually with mint
chutney, sliced onions, and a lemon wedge. Reheated tandoori chicken loses
most of what makes it good — the char turns rubbery and the smokiness fades.

## Tandoori Chicken vs. Other Popular Chicken Preparations

| Preparation | Cooking Method | Oil Used | Typical Texture | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tandoori Chicken** | Clay tandoor, 450°C | None (marinade only) | Charred outside, juicy inside | Those who want authentic, lower-oil flavour |
| **Chicken Tikka** | Same tandoor, boneless cubes | None | Firmer, bite-sized, smoky | Sharing plates, rolls |
| **Fried Chicken** | Deep-fried in oil | High | Crisp, oily crust | Comfort food, indulgence |
| **Butter Chicken** | Simmered in tomato-cream gravy (often starts as tandoori) | Moderate–high (gravy) | Rich, saucy | Pairing with naan/rice |

Chicken tikka is really just boneless tandoori chicken — same tandoor, same
marinade family, cut into smaller pieces so it cooks faster and works well in
[rolls](/chicken-roll-noida).

## Expert Tips for Ordering Tandoori Chicken

- **Ask how long it's marinated.** Under 2 hours usually means under-seasoned meat.
- **Check if it's cooked to order.** Pre-cooked tandoori chicken reheated on a grill loses its char and turns dry.
- **Classic vs. Creamy isn't about heat.** "Creamy" usually means a milder, cashew-yoghurt marinade — not spicier.
- **Bone-in has more flavour; boneless (tikka) is easier to share.** Order both if you're a group.
- **Pair it right.** Rumali roti or a simple onion salad lets the char and spice stand out — don't drown it in heavy gravy.
